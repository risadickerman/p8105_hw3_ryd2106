Before creating a table of gender vs education level, I first created a
gender data frame that would be used for all problem 2 related graphs
and tables. For this dataframe, I ensured legibility by renaming the
numerical factors of sex and education to their corresponding literal
labels. I then ensured that the education level followed a linear
progression from less than a highschool education to more than a
highschool education. This gender table shows the counts of each
participant by gender and education level. The majority of the
participant are in the highest education level possibly leading to
skewed end results. Except for higschool level, with a slight skew
towards men, there is almost no difference in the gender of the
participant in each education level.

To create the gender vs education table, I piped in the gender
dataframe. I then ensured that no seqn value was double counted by
passing it into the distinct function. Finally the resulting data was
piped into the tabyl function to create a reader friendly table of
counts of education level by sex. The resulting table lists counts of
participants by gender and education level.

To create a histogram of the number of age distributions for men and
women in each education category, I first piped in the gender data frame
and ensured unique counts of the participant by their seqn id. I then
created a histogram of the resultant data. I specified that it must be a
histogram. Faceting by sex and education, I created a side by side
comparison of these distributions. For aesthetics I converted the theme
to be more minimal, widened the bins, filled the inside white and
colored the outsides blue. The resulting diagram shows 6 separate
histograms showing the counts of participants by age. There are 2 rows,
delimited by gender, and 3 columns delimited by education level. The
color scheme matches the school spirit. This histogram reaffirms that
there are higher counts of the highest education level in this study. In
lower education sections there are sparser age categories. There seems
to be more of a balance between gender.

To create a plot comparing total activity level by age, with a further
comparison by sex and education, I piped in the previously created
gender data frame. From this I aggregated the mims across the minutes of
the day to create a total activity level variable. To ensure that no
seqn values were double counted, I passed the resulting table through
the distinct function. I then created a scatterplot of the total
activity vs age of the participant. This plot was faceted by education
and a comparison of gender was done by coloring by this variable. I
finally added a title, and renamed the x/y axes. This plot shows the
relation between total activity and the age of the participant. An
additional comparison is made across sex and education level. A clear
decrease of total activity can be seen with the increasing age. This
decrease is steepest within lower education levels, with a more gradual
decrease for more educate participants. While sex is closer together in
the least educated, the gender dichotomy is more apparent as the
education level progresses. Females were found to have higher activity
levels compared to their male counterparts in these higher education
levels.
